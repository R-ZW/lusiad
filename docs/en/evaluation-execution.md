[📊 Evaluation](evaluation.md)

The evaluation took place from **November 2 to 12, 2026**. A walkthrough and two pilots preceded the main collection. Those earlier sessions were used to check the procedure and improve how participants accessed the tool.

## 1. Walkthrough and pilots

The author ran through the full planned session before the pilots, completing the tasks and reviewing the materials. The walkthrough led to no adjustments. Two people outside the main study then took part in pilot sessions. Their responses were excluded from the main results.

| Pilot observation | Adjustment for the main sessions |
| --- | --- |
| Installing and configuring the tool on the participant's computer took effort and could delay the start. | The researcher hosted Lusiad and provided access through the participant's browser using a temporary link generated by `cloudflared`. |
| Participants sometimes had trouble identifying where a feature was located. | Guided tutorials became available from the help buttons in the interface. |

Moving installation to the researcher's computer let the main sessions focus on the modeling and tracking workflows. Experience opening the hosted application is evidence about that access arrangement. [Session guide](../zenodo/evaluation-material-session-guide.md)

## 2. Main sessions

The 15 main sessions took place individually over Google Meet from **November 8 to 12, 2026**. Participants shared their screens and authorized recording. The tool version, prepared Java project, and task statements were consistent across sessions.

1. The moderator explained the study and recording, shared the consent and profile form, and assigned a session code.
2. After the profile form, the moderator shared the tool and task links. Participants read one task at a time and demonstrated the requested state or entered factual answers where required.
3. The moderator asked participants to verbalize difficulties, feedback, and suggestions as they worked. Participants could consult the help tutorials, ask for clarification, and receive hints if they got stuck.
4. After the practical work, participants completed the SUS and TAM-inspired questionnaire. The script asked them to consider difficulties and incomplete activities and invited both favorable and unfavorable assessments. [Moderator script](../zenodo/evaluation-material-session-guide.md)

## 3. From collection to analysis

The electronic forms produced 15 records for each of the three instruments. Participant codes connected the profile, task form, questionnaire, and session material. Observed operations and the source they produced answer the modeling question. Factual answers support the tracking question and were scored using semantic equivalence. The SUS items were converted to scores, and usefulness and intention responses were tabulated by item. Open comments and anonymized transcripts contextualized those observations.
